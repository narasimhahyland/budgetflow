Capabilities

What BudgetFlow brings, wave by wave

BudgetFlow Enterprise is delivered in seven release waves. Each wave puts a complete, usable piece of the product in people's hands, and each one builds on the wave before it.

This page describes what the business can do at every stage: what is working today, what was added most recently, and what is still to come. Status is as of 12 November 2026.

Wave 1

Delivered

One set of books, one organization, one way in

Before any budget can be trusted, the company needs a record of the money that cannot be quietly edited, an accurate picture of its own structure, and a reliable answer to "who is this person and what may they see?". Wave 1 delivers all three.

A record of the money that holds up

  • Every amount is tracked in separate states: approved, passed down to a team, set aside, committed to a supplier and actually spent. "Available" means the same thing everywhere in the company.
  • Entries are added, never altered. A mistake is corrected by a reversing entry that stays visible, the way accountants expect.
  • A nightly check rebuilds every balance from scratch and compares it with what the product is showing. A difference is raised, not hidden.
  • Amounts are held exactly, in their own currency, and exchange-rate movement never silently eats someone's budget.

A complete, tamper-evident history

  • Every change records who made it, what changed, when, why, and the values before and after.
  • The history is chained so that a removed or altered entry can be detected. It is evidence, not a convenience log.
  • People see only the budgets and areas their role allows, and that limit is applied in one place rather than screen by screen.

Sign-in, the organization and finance basics

  • Sign in with the company's single sign-on, with multi-factor verification and self-service password reset.
  • The organization as it really is: regions, departments, teams, locations, cost centres, legal entities and reporting lines, each with the dates it was true, so last year's figures stay correct after a reorganization.
  • A people directory with headcount that can be loaded from the HR system, which is what makes per-person budgets possible later.
  • Finance set-up: fiscal calendars (including 4-4-5 and 52/53-week years), open and closed periods, currencies and exchange rates with the planning rate locked for the year, accounts and category mappings, reason codes, the supplier list and the approval-limit table.

Who it helps: finance and administrators get a system of record they can defend; auditors get a history they can rely on; everybody else gets correct numbers without knowing why.

Wave 2

Delivered

Budgets that flow down, and spend that is accounted for

This is the core promise of the product: leadership allocates once at the top, the money flows down through the organization with a check at every step, events get funded (including by more than one leader), and real spend is matched back to the right budget.

Planning and allocation

  • Build the annual budget from a template, import it from a spreadsheet with column mapping, or roll last year's structure forward.
  • Allocate to regions, departments, teams and locations one at a time, through a grid you can paste into from Excel, or by a rate per eligible employee that recalculates as people join and leave, with a top-up when headcount moves.
  • Over-allocation is refused at the moment it is attempted, including when two people allocate from the same budget at the same time.
  • Spread a budget across the months it will actually be spent, keep plan versions side by side, compare versions, and set spending limits and alert thresholds per budget.

Leadership controls that respect commitments

  • Freeze a budget, reduce it, or set it to zero, in one step and always with a reason.
  • Before confirming, see the impact: which teams are affected, what is already committed, and which requests are waiting.
  • Money already committed is honoured; new spending stops. A reduction hands back the waiting requests that no longer fit, with the reason, so nobody is left guessing.
  • Move budget between teams, ask for an increase, and release money from a central contingency reserve through approval rather than a side conversation.

Events, requests and co-funding

  • Plan events that span several locations, with the cost split fairly by location and team, and the supporting documents attached.
  • The Inter-Director Budget Contribution Request lets one leader formally ask another to fund part of an event. The contributor approves in full, approves part, or declines with a reason, and a live board shows each contributor's share, the spend against it and what is returned at the end.
  • Spending requests, additional-fund requests and over-budget requests all route to the right approver, who sees the budget left after approval and why the request came to them.
  • Approvers can act straight from the email, within the same limits.

Real spend, matched and reconciled

  • Card, expense and finance feeds are brought in and matched automatically to the right budget and event. What cannot be matched goes to a short review queue rather than disappearing.
  • Refunds, credit notes and reversals are handled properly, and the same transaction arriving twice is caught.
  • Finance signs off each month against the general ledger, entity by entity and account by account, and can record accruals and prepaid costs.
  • Spend in another currency is converted at the rate locked for the plan; the exchange effect is reported on its own and never consumes the budget.

Who it helps: the CEO and executives set and protect the plan; finance reconciles without chasing spreadsheets; budget owners and regional managers see what they have and delegate it; event organisers get funded and know where they stand; employees can ask for what they need.

Wave 3

Delivered

Policy that enforces itself, wherever people work

Spending policy stops being tribal knowledge and becomes rules the product applies and can explain. Approvals come to people in Slack, Teams, email and on a phone. And events get the planning depth that real events need.

Rules and approval routing

  • Set policy for spending limits, categories (including gifts, entertainment and alcohol), locations, suppliers, going over budget, frozen budgets, and how reminders and escalations work.
  • When several rules apply, the strictest one decides, and every rule that matched is shown alongside it, so a decision can always be explained. Since this week the rules run on every real request at submission and approval, and each decision records the rule versions and facts behind it.
  • Try a rule before it goes live: test it against real past requests and see what would have changed, without changing anything.
  • A draft that contradicts a live rule cannot be published. A published version cannot be quietly edited: it is replaced or retired, and the history is kept.

Approval hygiene people cannot work around

  • Delegation while away is controlled: not to yourself, not above your own limit, and not open-ended.
  • Changing somebody's approval limit needs a second person; the person who proposed it cannot approve it.
  • Approvals that go overdue escalate to the next level of authority, and that is recorded.
  • A rejected request comes back with the reason, and an approver's counter-offer can be accepted in one step. Auditors can read every decision exactly as it happened.

Slack, Teams, email and the phone

  • Approvers act on a request directly from Slack or Microsoft Teams, and the limits still apply: the decision is made by BudgetFlow, not by the card in the chat window.
  • Rejecting needs a reason. Large amounts cannot be waved through with one tap and are sent back to the product for a proper decision.
  • A faked or out-of-date chat message changes nothing, and the same message arriving twice decides once.
  • Check a balance or raise a quick request from chat, inside your own scope and respecting frozen budgets.
  • Work from a phone in the browser: approvals, balances, photographing a receipt with a queue for weak signal, and an alerts list. Push notifications to the phone are still to come (see Pending).
  • Notifications and tasks in one place, with comments, mentions that never widen who can see something, following, per-role digests and quiet hours. An approval assigned to you can never be switched off.

Events, properly organised

  • Templates, recurring series and cloning, so the quarterly social is not planned from scratch each time.
  • A shared checklist, tasks and an agenda, with items that complete themselves as quotes, invitations and attendance come in.
  • Invitations and RSVP with capacity and a waiting list, and a calendar view of who is coming by location.
  • Attendee needs (dietary, allergies, accessibility, travel, photo consent) collected privately: organisers and the person see the detail, everyone else sees counts.
  • Attendance on the day, including walk-ins, which recalculates the real cost per person and the split between contributors.
  • Supplier quotes compared side by side, with a written reason required when the cheapest is not chosen.
  • Afterwards: an anonymous survey that only reports once enough people have replied, a private photo gallery, and a scorecard showing cost per attendee, participation, no-shows and surplus returned.

Who it helps: finance writes policy once instead of repeating it; approvers decide in seconds from wherever they are; event organisers get planning tools instead of spreadsheets; employees are invited and heard; auditors can see how every decision was reached.

Wave 4

Delivered, with gaps

Seeing the whole picture, closing the books, connecting the systems

Wave 4 turns the recorded money into the reports leadership and finance act on, closes periods and the financial year properly, and plugs BudgetFlow into the systems the company already runs.

Dashboards and reports

  • An executive view of company budget health, drilling from the company total down to a single event or line. Home opens on an overview that fits the person's role.
  • A finance controller view of what still needs attention before close, and alerts and exceptions in one queue, each figure equal to its source and limited to the viewer's scope.
  • Standard reports: budget versus actual, allocation, utilization, location and department comparison, event spending, unused and expiring budget, and forecast versus variance.
  • Fairness and reach, side by side: spend per person by location, and how many people each location's activities actually involved.
  • Every report reconciles to the same underlying record, so two reports cannot disagree.

Board packs and exports

  • A board and planning pack assembled from the figures rather than by hand.
  • Reports downloaded as real CSV, Excel or PDF files of exactly what is on screen, with the same filters and permissions, through a short-lived link, and each export recorded in the history. Spreadsheet cells cannot smuggle in formulas.
  • Reports scheduled on a timetable, with the files attached to the email for recipients allowed to see them.

Period close and year-end

  • Close and lock a period so the numbers that went to the board cannot drift afterwards.
  • Forecast cycles where owners explain in their own words why their figures moved.
  • A year-end run with clear rules for what carries forward, what lapses and what is swept back to the centre.
  • Tax handled honestly: sales tax that can be recovered is not counted against the budget, and tax that cannot be recovered is. Tax rules are versioned and published by a tax approver. The shipped rates are sample values for finance to replace.
  • An employee benefit ledger that records each person's share of events and awards and flags benefit-in-kind thresholds, exactly at the limit and one unit over.

Connected systems and a public interface

  • Connections to HR, the general ledger, planning, expense and procurement, corporate cards, calendars and identity, each with a visible health status, retries and an error queue. They are built and checked against simulated providers; no live company system has been connected yet.
  • Duplicate spend arriving from two sources (a card charge and the expense claim that itemises it) is held for a person to decide which one is the record.
  • Event codes pushed to the card and expense tools so spend is tagged where it happens, and approved amounts optionally pushed to card limits, with failed pushes shown for a person to retry.
  • A Navan connector built to Navan's documented interface: actuals brought in, event codes offered at the point of spend, and over-budget exceptions queued for a person. It is proven against a simulated Navan; the live connection is switched off until it is checked against a real test account.
  • A documented interface for the company's own automation, with keys that expire, signed notifications of events and a delivery log.

Still to come in this wave's area

  • Pending approvals, approval performance and product analytics dashboards, and the one-click evidence pack In progress. They are built and reviewed, but not yet in the main product.
  • The payroll, intercompany recharge and reclass exports that follow from the tax and benefit ledgers Planned
  • The Navan live connection checked against a real test account Planned
  • Campaigns to reclaim and redistribute unused budget, and burn-rate alerts Planned
  • Applying a planning-system import to the budgets (today it is preview only), calendar RSVP sync, the supplier list from the ERP, and an approved request handed off as a prefilled expense claim Planned
  • A developer portal with public documentation and a sample-data sandbox Planned

Who it helps: the CEO and executives get the board view; finance closes the month and the year with less manual work; analysts get trustworthy numbers and real export files; IT connects BudgetFlow without custom glue.

Wave 5

Delivered, with gaps

Assistance that suggests, and a product that explains itself

Wave 5 adds help with the questions people currently send to finance, early warnings before money is wasted, and the administration settings that let the company run BudgetFlow its own way.

Answers and early warnings

  • Ask in plain language: how much is left, what is committed but not yet spent, where a request has got to, which budgets will lapse. Answers come from BudgetFlow's own figures, limited to what the asker may see, with every number traceable to its record.
  • The same questions can be asked in Slack and Teams, with private answers.
  • Owners can see early when a budget is heading for an overrun or for money left unused, based on the rate of spend, what is committed, the requests in the pipeline and last year's seasonal pattern, with a concrete next step they can take themselves. Today the check runs when asked; a nightly scan is planned.
  • A plain-English explanation of why a request was blocked or needs an extra approval, and what is possible within policy. The rules still decide; the assistant only explains.

Less typing, fewer surprises

  • Suggested splits of a budget among teams, based on eligible headcount, how much each used last year and how many people they reached, with the reasoning shown. The owner adjusts and submits it as a normal allocation.
  • Help placing the spend lines that automatic matching could not, so finance confirms in seconds rather than searching.
  • Receipts and quotes read automatically so forms arrive prefilled to check.
  • Flags on things that look unusual before money is committed: possible split requests, duplicates, a cost per person far above comparable events. Reused receipts and unusual suppliers are not flagged yet.
  • Assistance suggests; people decide. Nothing here approves a request or moves money, and every suggestion is accepted through the normal, audited action.
  • Since this week the assistance runs on a live AI model (Amazon Bedrock), behind the same guards: only permitted data is sent, every answer is checked against its expected shape, and figures must trace to a record. The assistance checks passed in a live local run; the screen journeys for these features are not all passing yet, and the automated pipeline skips AI checks because it has no model access.

Running the product

  • A guided set-up checklist worked out from what is already configured, and invitations that activate hundreds of budget owners in one pass.
  • Programs named the way employees know them ("Team Spirit" rather than a budget code), with the company's branding on the emails they receive.
  • Optional capabilities switched on or off, tried first with a pilot group, with an extra confirmation for anything affecting money or approvals. Switching a capability on never grants anyone a permission they did not have.
  • Company-wide settings for notifications, documents, data retention and legal holds, and security, plus a view of whether the service itself is healthy. The retention periods ship as provisional: finance and legal have not signed them off, and the product says so.
  • Each person gets their own activity history: what they asked for, what they approved, what they attended and how much of their yearly allowance they have used.

Still to come in this wave's area

  • Every assistance screen journey passing on the live model, and AI checks in the automated pipeline Planned
  • Nightly outlook and anomaly scans, and a tab for answers people reported as wrong Planned
  • An onboarding wizard, a permission matrix, personal profile and preferences, global search, editable categories and custom fields, the integrity-checks tab, and a "your team's budget" card for employees Planned

Who it helps: budget owners stop guessing and stop queueing at finance's door; finance answers fewer repeat questions; administrators configure the product without a project; employees understand what is available to them.

Wave 6

Delivered, with gaps

Deeper decisions, recognition, an open ecosystem

Wave 6 extends the product beyond controlling money into deciding well and showing what the money achieved, and opens BudgetFlow to the other tools the company and its partners use. The extra event-planning tools in the plan are not built yet.

Decide before you commit

  • Try a mid-year cut or reallocation privately first: see which events and commitments would be at risk, how spend per person by location would change, compare a few options against today, and then carry the chosen one out through the normal controls, reasons and approvals.
  • Monthly leadership summaries and owner commentary drafted from the figures, with every number linked to its source, for a person to check, edit and approve.
  • Outcome linkage, handled carefully: how attendees rate events, which kinds of event score best for their cost, and whether taking part relates to people staying. Shown as a relationship, never about an individual, and only for groups large enough that nobody can be singled out.

Recognition and allowances

  • Recognition awards and per-person wellbeing and learning allowances paid from a real budget rather than leaking into expense claims. A frozen programme budget refuses new claims while claims already in flight complete.
  • A peer award needs a second person, who can be neither the giver nor the recipient, and managers give within a quarterly allowance.
  • Friendly competition between offices that ranks taking part, never spending.

An open ecosystem

  • Live BudgetFlow figures in Power BI or Tableau, where each connection carries the scope of the person who made it, so a report never shows more than that person could see in the product.
  • No-code connections to the long tail of tools teams use, while decisions about money stay with a person inside BudgetFlow. Automations triggered by BudgetFlow events are listed but not yet delivered, until each delivery can be limited to what its owner may see.
  • Budget questions answerable from the AI assistant somebody already uses, read-only and under their own permissions.
  • A partner marketplace for team experiences, catering, gifts and kits, where a booking becomes a quote and then a committed amount through the normal approval path.
  • "Your team's budget" and upcoming events embedded in the intranet or HR portal, each viewer seeing only their own.

Still to come in this wave's area

  • Event depth: a planning assistant that starts from past events, local holidays on the calendar, date and activity polls with an idea board, quick check-in at the door, and funded remote participation Not built
  • Population-level warning signs for finance and audit (amounts just under approval thresholds, approvals given within seconds). Waiting on approval timing being recorded first Not built
  • Withholding tax on awards, organiser badges, and a quarterly organiser summary for managers Not built
  • An installable phone app with real push notifications Planned. Native iOS and Android app-store wrappers are deferred by decision; the phone pages already work in the browser.

Who it helps: executives test a decision before making it; HR and people teams can show what engagement spending achieved; employees get recognition and allowances paid from a real budget; analysts and IT get the data where they already work.

Wave 7

Delivered

Proving it, securing it, writing it down

The final wave added no new features. It is the assurance pass that lets the company put the product in front of an auditor, a security reviewer or a new joiner with confidence. It also produced the honest list of what the plan asks for and the product does not yet do, which is the Pending list below.

Quality

  • Every screen in the product reached by a check that uses it as a real person would: 112 of 112.
  • Performance measured on the sample data and on about 20,000 ledger entries: every request succeeded, and two summary views slow down as the data grows.
  • Upgrade checks that move a database from empty, and from the previous release with data in it, to the current release without losing anything.
  • The nightly balance rebuild and the tamper-evident history checked at volume, and found clean.

Security

  • A security review of access, credentials, exports and the release process. All six findings are fixed and proven, including a way round the allowed-network limits on keys and spreadsheet formula injection in exports.
  • A deployment refuses to start with the publicly known development secrets.
  • A controlled, approved, read-only, time-limited and fully audited procedure for the rare case where a platform operator must look at company financial data.

Evidence and documentation

  • Business rules mapped to checks that prove them on the running product, with the results recorded rather than asserted: 112 rules today.
  • Every check run on a freshly loaded product: 627 checks of the interface and business outcomes, 590 user journeys across desktop, tablet and phone, the acceptance scenario, and a picture of every screen state compared with its approved version.
  • An evidence pack for every phase, a final report, and refreshed documentation for the people who use the product and the people who run it.
  • Every one of the plan's 119 catalogued features traced to a passing check, a gap package or a recorded deferral (#125): 72 proven, 14 built but not yet proven, 19 gaps, 14 deferred.

Still to come in this wave's area

  • Checks for the 14 features that are built but not yet proven Planned
  • Performance at the plan's full scale (50,000 budgets, 500,000 ledger entries), and upgrade checks from every earlier release Planned
  • Dedicated checks for each simulated connector and for a few areas proven only once (board pack currency effects, the approval routing builder) Planned

Who it helps: auditors and security reviewers get what they need without a special project; administrators and new joiners get answers from documentation; leadership gets a clear account of what is proven and what is not.

What "delivered" means on this page

Delivered means the capability is in the product and has been proven working end to end against the running system, as the people whose job it is, with the results recorded.

Delivered, with gaps means the wave is in the product and proven, but parts of its plan are not built yet; they are listed in a dashed box at the end of the wave.

In progress means the work has started and is not yet in the main product. Planned means it is specified and scheduled, but not started. Not built means it is in the plan with no work scheduled yet.

Nothing on this page is described as delivered on the strength of a plan or an open pull request alone.

What does not change between waves

Money is recorded to accounting standards and corrected by reversal, never by editing. Approvals happen within the limits set for each person's role. Everyone sees only their own part of the organization. Every change leaves a tamper-evident record.

Assistance features suggest, flag and explain. Every approval, transfer and control is made by a person, within their own limits, through the same audited action they would use anyway.

In short
